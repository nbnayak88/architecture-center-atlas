# The successor on the slide cannot run the plant
## A two-hour Origin Story sprint: Architecting Growth & Leadership

**SuccessLabs Academy · HR04 · Energy · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**Platform:** SAP · **Domain:** Human Capital Management · **Architecture:** Applied Application Architecture (AA)  
**Lab:** IIL — Implementation Intelligence Lab · **Intent:** Build  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-HR04-ENE-sprint · Free — Community/Citizen Edition  
**Version:** 2026-10-19 · Published lesson; not an enrolled course or certification.

> **Evolution lens:** replace → assess → connect → develop → assist. New technology changes how work is coordinated; it does not remove responsibility for evidence, people impact and authorized decisions.

## 1. What this two-hour sprint achieves

By the end you will:
1. Explain the general origins and purpose of this domain before naming a product.
2. Reconstruct its process evolution across **1975–2026**, identifying what improved and what remained unresolved.
3. Compare **2030, 2035, 2040 and 2050** scenarios, with a competing future and a validation question.
4. Map application responsibilities and source objects for the OmniVerse Energy case.
5. Produce a synthetic build prototype, run three tabletop tests and defend an evidence-based next step.

**The 20% to master:** critical-role definition; evidence-backed readiness; technical and management pathways; knowledge transfer; fair nominations.

**Deliverable:** one Domain Evolution Build Card containing a timeline, persona needs, ownership map, state/control prototype, option decision and test evidence. Practical capability here means implementation preparation; it does not imply production configuration proficiency after two hours.

## 2. Origins and purpose — the general story

Succession began as continuity planning: who could take over when a leader or expert left? Replacement charts made names visible, but a nominated person was not necessarily ready. Career development broadens the question to what experience, knowledge and support make future contribution possible. Technical careers matter alongside management careers.

**Teach the decision first:** what decision is needed, whose outcome matters, what evidence supports it, which application is responsible, and who has authority to act?

### Fifty years of process evolution

Dates group broad historical patterns for learning. They do not claim invention dates, uniform adoption or a source-proven worldwide chronology. Paper, legacy, cloud and assisted processes can coexist today; the table is an educational synthesis.

| Era | Process pattern | What improved | Remaining gap | Energy-sector example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1975–1984 | Replacement charts and manager nominations | Named backups for senior positions | Relationships substitute for verified readiness | An LNG expert has a backup on paper but no transferred troubleshooting knowledge |
| 1985–1994 | Competency frameworks and development centres | More explicit criteria and development needs | Potential labels may remain subjective | Compare technical mastery with managerial potential without conflating them |
| 1995–2004 | Electronic talent inventories and succession records | Searchable candidates and visible vacancies | Incomplete records can hide informal experts | Record rare refinery knowledge, not just leadership grades |
| 2005–2014 | Integrated talent reviews and career plans | Performance, learning and succession can inform one another | Past rating does not prove next-role readiness | Agree the evidence needed for a larger technical remit |
| 2015–2019 | Career experiences, mentoring and internal opportunities | Development extends beyond classroom courses | Opportunity access can remain uneven | Nadia needs an assessed assignment, not another generic nomination |
| 2020–2022 | Remote mentoring and continuity under disruption | Broader access to experts and recorded knowledge | Tacit practice remains hard to transmit | Remote discussion supplements supervised LNG practice |
| 2023–2026 | Skills inference and assisted talent discovery | Wider candidate discovery and draft development paths | Inferred skills are not validated readiness | Human panels challenge labels and assess technical continuity |

**Evolution activity:** cover the table. For each era, say “the process changed from ___ to ___ because ___; the unresolved problem was ___.” Use an energy example rather than a product slogan.

**Historical caution:** the 2020–2022 row describes possible process pressures/responses in distributed work, not proof that every energy enterprise changed in that way. Modern product documentation supports current capabilities, not the full historical chronology.

## 3. Future perspectives to 2050

**Scenario boundary:** all four horizons are possible design directions. None is an official SAP roadmap, forecast, legal prediction or achieved OmniVerse result.

| Horizon | Possible process direction | What to validate before scaling |
|---|---|---|
| 2030 | Assisted slate discovery with cited evidence and human review | Test a bounded synthetic pilot; compare with a non-AI/manual option and preserve an accountable owner |
| 2035 | Verified work experiences and fair access to technical development | Validate evidence quality, worker access and role/country differences; do not infer authority from a recommendation |
| 2040 | Compare develop, recruit and redesign options for critical roles | Evaluate interoperability, practical operating value, worker impact and recovery under changing requirements |
| 2050 | A possible continuous capability-continuity ecosystem across energy technologies | Retain human challenge, explainable responsibility, accessible alternatives and evidence history; revise when assumptions fail |

| Alternative 2050 world | What could happen | Design choice useful today |
|---|---|---|
| Trusted learning ecosystem | People, accountable owners and bounded assistance improve the capability together | Inspectable evidence, worker voice and measured experiments |
| Opaque metric/automation regime | Convenient scores or actions displace context and responsibility | Explicit prohibitions, review gates, correction and fallback |
| Fragmented local ecosystems | Different applications, definitions and institutions remain incompatible | Shared semantics and acceptance contracts with justified local variants |

**2050 experiment question:** which part of the future could you test with today's synthetic records? What observation would make you abandon the proposed design?

## 6. Key concepts and application responsibility

| Concept | Meaning and boundary |
|---|---|
| Critical role | A role whose loss creates material continuity risk; not simply a senior grade |
| Successor | A potential candidate, not a guaranteed appointment |
| Readiness | Evidence-backed ability against a defined role and horizon |
| Potential | A hypothesis about growth, not current competence |
| Knowledge transfer | Practice and evidence of learning, not merely document upload |
| Development action | An owned experience or support step with review date |

| Application / accountable area | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Succession | Restricted nominations/readiness decisions and review history |
| EC / HR01 | Approved position, employment and organizational facts |
| Learning / HR06 | Learning and assessed proficiency evidence |
| Business role owner | Confirms operational readiness against role requirements |
| Recruiting / HR02 | External hiring when approved continuity options require it |

**Source information objects:** critical position; readiness horizon; evidence; development action; knowledge-transfer record. Record attribute owner, authoritative source, purpose, access, retention, effective date and quality rule.

**Source value stream:** Identify critical role → assess risk → nominate → validate readiness → develop → rehearse handover. Define a stakeholder-valued exit criterion at each stage before detailed process modeling.

A capability is an ability the organization needs; the value stream describes progression toward stakeholder value. Neither is simply a list of applications.

**AA boundary:** this sprint assigns application responsibility and acceptance criteria. Reference data stewardship, security, integration, UX, infrastructure and AI concerns without assuming the application view alone implements those controls. Cross-domain consumers must not silently become owners of source facts.

## 8. Build experiment — a small, reviewable implementation design

Use **two synthetic records**, one routine and one variant drawn from the source challenges. Assign teaching identifiers **HR04-SYN-001/002**. Do not use real employee information, internal endpoints or live banking/access actions.

| Build Card field | What you must write |
|---|---|
| Purpose/population | Decision, affected person/role and intended outcome; identify missing facts |
| Two variants | Unsupported readiness label versus Retirement before successor readiness |
| Objects and time | Identifiers, source, relevant validity/as-of/processing dates; distinguish their meanings |
| Lifecycle/gates | Proposed from-state → permitted transition → prerequisite → accountable actor |
| Evidence | Source reference, validation status, version and correction owner |
| Application boundary | Succession responsibility versus Recruiting / HR02 |
| Global/local decision | Shared principle, justified role/country variant and evidence still needed |
| Human and automation boundary | One permitted administrative task; one protected consequential decision |
| Exception/recovery | Visible exception, owner, fallback, recovery and confirmation |
| Architecture decision | Compare a replacement chart, independent business nominations and common critical-role/readiness principles with governed role variants. |
| Measure | Definition, denominator, exclusions, as-of date, owner and interpretation limits |
| Test evidence | Input → expected → actual paper result → correction → evidence note → pass/revise |

**Conceptual workflow:** establish approved source evidence → validate → apply agreed rule → authorized decision/action → confirm downstream business outcome → review exceptions. These are teaching stages, not prescribed SAP object/status names. Choose actual module configuration only after validating the contract and product version.

### Three tabletop tests

| Test | Challenge | Expected result in this proposed design |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Unsupported readiness label | Require criteria, evidence, owner and review; do not present two years as an objective fact |
| T2 | Retirement before successor readiness | Document interim coverage, supervised transfer and accountable risk acceptance; no unsupported ready-now claim |
| T3 | Narrow nomination network | Review access and exclusion; expand evidence search without promising appointment |

**How to run:** represent each record/state on paper or in a simple table. Execute the steps yourself; record whether the initial design produced the expected outcome. If it did not, revise and rerun. Designed expectations are not actual SAP test results.

**Acceptance:** explain the timeline, defend one 2050 hypothesis against an alternative, distinguish the concepts, assign owners and satisfy all three tabletop expectations after documented revision. An unsupported safety/pay/access/employee-rights outcome requires redesign. List any control that remains untested.

**Source measure:** Critical-role coverage = critical positions with evidence-backed successors / critical positions; split ready-now versus developing. Owner: domain lead unless specified; cadence: proposed pilot review and monthly follow-up. Establish denominator, exclusions, as-of date and baseline before setting a numeric target. Original source “Victory Measures” are targets, not achievements.

Do not fabricate a baseline, achieved benefit or target. A successful prototype proves only the limited behaviour you actually exercised.
