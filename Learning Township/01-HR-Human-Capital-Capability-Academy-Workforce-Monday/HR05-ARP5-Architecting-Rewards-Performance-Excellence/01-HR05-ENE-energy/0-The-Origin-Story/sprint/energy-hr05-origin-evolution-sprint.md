## 1. What this two-hour sprint achieves

By the end you will:
1. Explain the general origins and purpose of this domain before naming a product.
2. Reconstruct its process evolution across **1975–2026**, identifying what improved and what remained unresolved.
3. Compare **2030, 2035, 2040 and 2050** scenarios, with a competing future and a validation question.
4. Map application responsibilities and source objects for the OmniVerse Energy case.
5. Produce a synthetic build prototype, run three tabletop tests and defend an evidence-based next step.

**The 20% to master:** base pay versus incentive; eligibility and service segments; goal weights and achievement; equity and policy gates; approved-to-paid reconciliation.

**Deliverable:** one Domain Evolution Build Card containing a timeline, persona needs, ownership map, state/control prototype, option decision and test evidence. Practical capability here means implementation preparation; it does not imply production configuration proficiency after two hours.

## 2. Origins and purpose — the general story

Reward administration grew around wages, grades, benefits and incentives. As organizations expanded, salary structures, market comparisons and variable incentives sought consistency and motivation. Modern total rewards connects affordability, contribution, employee needs and fairness. A planning worksheet proposes awards; payroll determines net pay under applicable rules.

**Teach the decision first:** what decision is needed, whose outcome matters, what evidence supports it, which application is responsible, and who has authority to act?

### Fifty years of process evolution

Dates group broad historical patterns for learning. They do not claim invention dates, uniform adoption or a source-proven worldwide chronology. Paper, legacy, cloud and assisted processes can coexist today; the table is an educational synthesis.

| Era | Process pattern | What improved | Remaining gap | Energy-sector example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1975–1984 | Grade-based pay and manual incentive administration | Predictable structures and documented awards | Local discretion and opaque comparisons | Site allowances coexist with basic salary |
| 1985–1994 | Market benchmarking and differentiated incentives | Greater attention to scarce skills and performance | Market data can be misapplied across roles | An offshore premium needs a stated purpose |
| 1995–2004 | Spreadsheets and HR/payroll records | Faster calculations and consolidation | Formula versions and currencies diverge | A transfer changes eligibility mid-cycle |
| 2005–2014 | Integrated compensation planning and incentive tools | Budgets, approvals and histories become more visible | Planning and actual payment can still disagree | Check approved awards against payroll inputs |
| 2015–2019 | Pay equity, transparency and balanced outcomes | More attention to fairness and nonfinancial measures | A favourable weighted average can hide critical conduct | Avoid rewarding output that bypasses approved safety policy |
| 2020–2022 | Changing work patterns and compensation pressures | Organizations revisit allowances and employee needs | Rapid exceptions can undermine equity | Document why field and office treatment differs |
| 2023–2026 | Assisted recommendations and explanation | Potentially less drafting and comparison effort | Opaque recommendations and flawed inputs amplify inequity | Keep accountable approval, evidence and employee explanation |

**Evolution activity:** cover the table. For each era, say “the process changed from ___ to ___ because ___; the unresolved problem was ___.” Use an energy example rather than a product slogan.

**Historical caution:** the 2020–2022 row describes possible process pressures/responses in distributed work, not proof that every energy enterprise changed in that way. Modern product documentation supports current capabilities, not the full historical chronology.

## 3. Future perspectives to 2050

**Scenario boundary:** all four horizons are possible design directions. None is an official SAP roadmap, forecast, legal prediction or achieved OmniVerse result.

| Horizon | Possible process direction | What to validate before scaling |
|---|---|---|
| 2030 | Explainable planning assistance inside approved budgets | Test a bounded synthetic pilot; compare with a non-AI/manual option and preserve an accountable owner |
| 2035 | More transparent reward choices with governed local differences | Validate evidence quality, worker access and role/country differences; do not infer authority from a recommendation |
| 2040 | Reward transition skills and team contribution with tested evidence | Evaluate interoperability, practical operating value, worker impact and recovery under changing requirements |
| 2050 | A possible adaptable rewards ecosystem balancing fairness, affordability and long-term value | Retain human challenge, explainable responsibility, accessible alternatives and evidence history; revise when assumptions fail |

| Alternative 2050 world | What could happen | Design choice useful today |
|---|---|---|
| Trusted learning ecosystem | People, accountable owners and bounded assistance improve the capability together | Inspectable evidence, worker voice and measured experiments |
| Opaque metric/automation regime | Convenient scores or actions displace context and responsibility | Explicit prohibitions, review gates, correction and fallback |
| Fragmented local ecosystems | Different applications, definitions and institutions remain incompatible | Shared semantics and acceptance contracts with justified local variants |

**2050 experiment question:** which part of the future could you test with today's synthetic records? What observation would make you abandon the proposed design?

## 4. OmniVerse Energy — the decision and the people

**Fictional enterprise baseline:** OmniVerse Energy Ltd., 68,000 employees across 42 countries. “OmniEnergy” is an alias preserved in original research. Historical eras are not a corporate biography; OmniVerse was founded in 1978.

**Reward sustainable energy performance while protecting safety, equity, compliance, and affordability.**
OmniEnergy uses inconsistent salary structures, local spreadsheets, scarce-skill premiums, site allowances, shift payments, sales incentives, and executive plans. Some incentives favor production volume without adequately reflecting safety, emissions, quality, customer outcomes, or long-term asset performance.

## 6. Key concepts and application responsibility

| Concept | Meaning and boundary |
|---|---|
| Base pay | Recurring salary component; distinguish permanent changes from one-off awards |
| Variable pay | Policy-governed incentive linked to defined eligibility and results |
| Eligibility segment | A period/population qualifying under the approved plan |
| Weight/achievement | Relative contribution of a goal versus its measured result |
| Reverse metric | A measure where lower can be better; define approved achievement mapping |
| Equity review | Investigate comparable-role differences; a gap alone does not explain its cause |

| Application / accountable area | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Compensation | Approved base-pay planning process and recommendations |
| Variable Pay | Governed incentive design/calculation |
| Performance / HSE / operating owners | Approved performance inputs and validated domain facts |
| Payroll / HR07 | Gross-to-net and authorized payment processing |
| Finance | Budget, accrual and posting controls |

**Source information objects:** pay range; currency; eligible service segment; goal weight; achievement; payout; approval. Record attribute owner, authoritative source, purpose, access, retention, effective date and quality rule.

**Source value stream:** Budget → eligibility → recommendation → equity and safety review → approve → publish → reconcile. Define a stakeholder-valued exit criterion at each stage before detailed process modeling.

A capability is an ability the organization needs; the value stream describes progression toward stakeholder value. Neither is simply a list of applications.

**AA boundary:** this sprint assigns application responsibility and acceptance criteria. Reference data stewardship, security, integration, UX, infrastructure and AI concerns without assuming the application view alone implements those controls. Cross-domain consumers must not silently become owners of source facts.

## 8. Build experiment — a small, reviewable implementation design

Use **two synthetic records**, one routine and one variant drawn from the source challenges. Assign teaching identifiers **HR05-SYN-001/002**. Do not use real employee information, internal endpoints or live banking/access actions.

| Build Card field | What you must write |
|---|---|
| Purpose/population | Decision, affected person/role and intended outcome; identify missing facts |
| Two variants | Safety concern despite margin target versus Mid-period transfer and reverse metric |
| Objects and time | Identifiers, source, relevant validity/as-of/processing dates; distinguish their meanings |
| Lifecycle/gates | Proposed from-state → permitted transition → prerequisite → accountable actor |
| Evidence | Source reference, validation status, version and correction owner |
| Application boundary | Compensation responsibility versus Finance |
| Global/local decision | Shared principle, justified role/country variant and evidence still needed |
| Human and automation boundary | One permitted administrative task; one protected consequential decision |
| Exception/recovery | Visible exception, owner, fallback, recovery and confirmation |
| Architecture decision | Compare one global formula, independent local plans and common policy/control principles with justified market and agreement variants. |
| Measure | Definition, denominator, exclusions, as-of date, owner and interpretation limits |
| Test evidence | Input → expected → actual paper result → correction → evidence note → pass/revise |

**Conceptual workflow:** establish approved source evidence → validate → apply agreed rule → authorized decision/action → confirm downstream business outcome → review exceptions. These are teaching stages, not prescribed SAP object/status names. Choose actual module configuration only after validating the contract and product version.

### Three tabletop tests

| Test | Challenge | Expected result in this proposed design |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Safety concern despite margin target | Validate facts and apply approved policy; no automatic full payout or invented penalty |
| T2 | Mid-period transfer and reverse metric | Preserve service segments; apply approved achievement mapping once per applicable segment |
| T3 | Statement-to-payroll mismatch | Keep discrepancy visible; reconcile authorized amounts before claiming paid; use controlled correction |

**How to run:** represent each record/state on paper or in a simple table. Execute the steps yourself; record whether the initial design produced the expected outcome. If it did not, revise and rerun. Designed expectations are not actual SAP test results.

**Acceptance:** explain the timeline, defend one 2050 hypothesis against an alternative, distinguish the concepts, assign owners and satisfy all three tabletop expectations after documented revision. An unsupported safety/pay/access/employee-rights outcome requires redesign. List any control that remains untested.

**Source measure:** Approved-to-paid reconciliation = matched approved award amounts / total approved award amounts; pair with unexplained pay differences and planning time. Owner: domain lead unless specified; cadence: proposed pilot review and monthly follow-up. Establish denominator, exclusions, as-of date and baseline before setting a numeric target. Original source “Victory Measures” are targets, not achievements.

Do not fabricate a baseline, achieved benefit or target. A successful prototype proves only the limited behaviour you actually exercised.
