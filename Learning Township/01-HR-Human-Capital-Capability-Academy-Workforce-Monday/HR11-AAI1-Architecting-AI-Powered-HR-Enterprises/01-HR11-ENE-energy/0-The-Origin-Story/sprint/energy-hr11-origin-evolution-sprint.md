# The assistant answered confidently from the wrong policy
## A two-hour Origin Story sprint: Architecting AI-Powered HR Enterprises

**SuccessLabs Academy · HR11 · Energy · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**Platform:** SAP · **Domain:** Human Capital Management · **Architecture:** Applied Application Architecture (AA)  
**Lab:** IIL — Implementation Intelligence Lab · **Intent:** Build  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-HR11-ENE-sprint · Free — Community/Citizen Edition  
**Version:** 2026-10-19 · Published lesson; not an enrolled course or certification.

> **Evolution lens:** automate rules → analyze → predict → generate → govern actions. New technology changes how work is coordinated; it does not remove responsibility for evidence, people impact and authorized decisions.

## 1. What this two-hour sprint achieves

By the end you will:
1. Explain the general origins and purpose of this domain before naming a product.
2. Reconstruct its process evolution across **1975–2026**, identifying what improved and what remained unresolved.
3. Compare **2030, 2035, 2040 and 2050** scenarios, with a competing future and a validation question.
4. Map application responsibilities and source objects for the OmniVerse Energy case.
5. Produce a synthetic build prototype, run three tabletop tests and defend an evidence-based next step.

**The 20% to master:** rules versus learned models; grounding; evaluation; action authorization; human challenge and rollback.

**Deliverable:** one Domain Evolution Build Card containing a timeline, persona needs, ownership map, state/control prototype, option decision and test evidence. Practical capability here means implementation preparation; it does not imply production configuration proficiency after two hours.

## 2. Origins and purpose — the general story

HR automation first encoded rules and workflows; analytics and machine learning later supported prediction and matching. Generative systems can draft and converse, while tool-using assistants may act on applications. These are different capabilities and risks. Responsible workforce AI defines permitted purpose, evidence, authority, testing and recovery for each use case.

**Teach the decision first:** what decision is needed, whose outcome matters, what evidence supports it, which application is responsible, and who has authority to act?

### Fifty years of process evolution

Dates group broad historical patterns for learning. They do not claim invention dates, uniform adoption or a source-proven worldwide chronology. Paper, legacy, cloud and assisted processes can coexist today; the table is an educational synthesis.

| Era | Process pattern | What improved | Remaining gap | Energy-sector example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1975–1984 | Rules and administrative automation | Repeatable decisions for explicit conditions | Rules can encode mistaken policy | A workflow executes the rule it was given |
| 1985–1994 | Expert-system approaches and decision support | Explicit knowledge can assist structured tasks | Coverage and maintenance remain difficult | An exception outside the rule needs expert judgment |
| 1995–2004 | Workflow/HRIS automation and statistical analysis | More connected administration | Automated processing is not necessarily AI | Do not call every approval workflow intelligent |
| 2005–2014 | Machine learning for prediction and matching | Pattern discovery across larger datasets | Bias, proxies and unexplained scores | A retention score cannot justify adverse action |
| 2015–2019 | Conversational services and wider AI deployment | Potentially easier access to help | Limited scope and wrong answers remain | A bot needs a reliable escalation route |
| 2020–2022 | Greater digital reliance and governance attention | More need for oversight of data-driven work | Surveillance and sensitive inference risks | Worker trust cannot be inferred from usage |
| 2023–2026 | Generative and tool-using assistance | Drafting, retrieval and scoped actions | Hallucination, prompt injection and unauthorized changes | A current source, evaluator and action gate are essential |

**Evolution activity:** cover the table. For each era, say “the process changed from ___ to ___ because ___; the unresolved problem was ___.” Use an energy example rather than a product slogan.

**Historical caution:** the 2020–2022 row describes possible process pressures/responses in distributed work, not proof that every energy enterprise changed in that way. Modern product documentation supports current capabilities, not the full historical chronology.

## 3. Future perspectives to 2050

**Scenario boundary:** all four horizons are possible design directions. None is an official SAP roadmap, forecast, legal prediction or achieved OmniVerse result.

| Horizon | Possible process direction | What to validate before scaling |
|---|---|---|
| 2030 | Tested assistants for bounded low-risk tasks | Test a bounded synthetic pilot; compare with a non-AI/manual option and preserve an accountable owner |
| 2035 | Stronger provenance, consent/choice where applicable and scoped action controls | Validate evidence quality, worker access and role/country differences; do not infer authority from a recommendation |
| 2040 | Cross-application assistance governed by explicit decision rights | Evaluate interoperability, practical operating value, worker impact and recovery under changing requirements |
| 2050 | A possible accountable human–AI workplace with inspectable actions, appeal and recovery | Retain human challenge, explainable responsibility, accessible alternatives and evidence history; revise when assumptions fail |

| Alternative 2050 world | What could happen | Design choice useful today |
|---|---|---|
| Trusted learning ecosystem | People, accountable owners and bounded assistance improve the capability together | Inspectable evidence, worker voice and measured experiments |
| Opaque metric/automation regime | Convenient scores or actions displace context and responsibility | Explicit prohibitions, review gates, correction and fallback |
| Fragmented local ecosystems | Different applications, definitions and institutions remain incompatible | Shared semantics and acceptance contracts with justified local variants |

**2050 experiment question:** which part of the future could you test with today's synthetic records? What observation would make you abandon the proposed design?

## 4. OmniVerse Energy — the decision and the people

**Fictional enterprise baseline:** OmniVerse Energy Ltd., 68,000 employees across 42 countries. “OmniEnergy” is an alias preserved in original research. Historical eras are not a corporate biography; OmniVerse was founded in 1978.

**Augment employees, managers, and HR responsibly—without hiding consequential decisions inside automation.**
OmniEnergy wants AI assistance for employee service, recruiting, learning, managers, HR operations, workforce planning, and knowledge discovery. However, employment, safety, privacy, labor, and fairness risks require clear autonomy boundaries and practical controls.

**Scope note:** Consequence, permission and source quality determine allowed autonomy. A fluent answer or a successful tool call does not authorize an employment change or prove policy correctness.

## 6. Key concepts and application responsibility

| Concept | Meaning and boundary |
|---|---|
| Grounding | Use approved current sources; not a guarantee of correctness |
| Hallucination | Unsupported generated content |
| Evaluation | Representative tests with defined expected outcomes |
| Prompt injection | Untrusted content attempts to redirect system behaviour |
| Authorization | Separate permission for a specific action and scope |
| Rollback/containment | Stop unsafe use and restore controlled state with evidence |

| Application / accountable area | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Policy owner | Approves current policy and interpretation |
| AI service | Retrieves/drafts within allowed scope |
| HR transaction system | Owns approved employee facts and workflow |
| Human decision/action owner | Approves consequential outcomes |
| Security/model operations | Testing, access, monitoring, incident response and recovery |

**Source information objects:** policy version; retrieval reference; model version; evaluation set; action authorization; incident. Record attribute owner, authoritative source, purpose, access, retention, effective date and quality rule.

**Source value stream:** Propose → classify risk → ground → evaluate → approve → monitor → retire. Define a stakeholder-valued exit criterion at each stage before detailed process modeling.

A capability is an ability the organization needs; the value stream describes progression toward stakeholder value. Neither is simply a list of applications.

**AA boundary:** this sprint assigns application responsibility and acceptance criteria. Reference data stewardship, security, integration, UX, infrastructure and AI concerns without assuming the application view alone implements those controls. Cross-domain consumers must not silently become owners of source facts.

## 8. Build experiment — a small, reviewable implementation design

Use **two synthetic records**, one routine and one variant drawn from the source challenges. Assign teaching identifiers **HR11-SYN-001/002**. Do not use real employee information, internal endpoints or live banking/access actions.

| Build Card field | What you must write |
|---|---|
| Purpose/population | Decision, affected person/role and intended outcome; identify missing facts |
| Two variants | Hallucinated or stale policy versus Prompt injection / unauthorized correction |
| Objects and time | Identifiers, source, relevant validity/as-of/processing dates; distinguish their meanings |
| Lifecycle/gates | Proposed from-state → permitted transition → prerequisite → accountable actor |
| Evidence | Source reference, validation status, version and correction owner |
| Application boundary | Policy owner responsibility versus Security/model operations |
| Global/local decision | Shared principle, justified role/country variant and evidence still needed |
| Human and automation boundary | One permitted administrative task; one protected consequential decision |
| Exception/recovery | Visible exception, owner, fallback, recovery and confirmation |
| Architecture decision | Compare no AI for the use case, answer-only assistance and bounded tool execution; choose based on evidence and risk rather than an autonomy target. |
| Measure | Definition, denominator, exclusions, as-of date, owner and interpretation limits |
| Test evidence | Input → expected → actual paper result → correction → evidence note → pass/revise |

**Conceptual workflow:** establish approved source evidence → validate → apply agreed rule → authorized decision/action → confirm downstream business outcome → review exceptions. These are teaching stages, not prescribed SAP object/status names. Choose actual module configuration only after validating the contract and product version.

### Three tabletop tests

| Test | Challenge | Expected result in this proposed design |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Hallucinated or stale policy | Cite current approved evidence or decline/escalate; correction path visible |
| T2 | Prompt injection / unauthorized correction | Treat source text as untrusted; deny out-of-scope tool use and retain audit evidence |
| T3 | Missing logs / rollback failure | Block promotion to production for critical control gap; demonstrate containment and verified recovery |

**How to run:** represent each record/state on paper or in a simple table. Execute the steps yourself; record whether the initial design produced the expected outcome. If it did not, revise and rerun. Designed expectations are not actual SAP test results.

**Acceptance:** explain the timeline, defend one 2050 hypothesis against an alternative, distinguish the concepts, assign owners and satisfy all three tabletop expectations after documented revision. An unsupported safety/pay/access/employee-rights outcome requires redesign. List any control that remains untested.

**Source measure:** Grounded-answer correctness = answers supported by current approved evidence / evaluated answers; pair with escalation success and unauthorized-action count. Owner: domain lead unless specified; cadence: proposed pilot review and monthly follow-up. Establish denominator, exclusions, as-of date and baseline before setting a numeric target. Original source “Victory Measures” are targets, not achievements.

Do not fabricate a baseline, achieved benefit or target. A successful prototype proves only the limited behaviour you actually exercised.
