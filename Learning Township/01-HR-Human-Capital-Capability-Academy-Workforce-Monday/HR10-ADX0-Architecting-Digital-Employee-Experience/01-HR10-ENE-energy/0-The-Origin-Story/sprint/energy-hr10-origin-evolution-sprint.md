# One front door still needs a human behind it
## A two-hour Origin Story sprint: Architecting Digital Employee Experience

**SuccessLabs Academy · HR10 · Energy · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**Platform:** SAP · **Domain:** Human Capital Management · **Architecture:** Applied Application Architecture (AA)  
**Lab:** IIL — Implementation Intelligence Lab · **Intent:** Build  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-HR10-ENE-sprint · Free — Community/Citizen Edition  
**Version:** 2026-10-19 · Published lesson; not an enrolled course or certification.

> **Evolution lens:** inform → transact → portal → include → assist. New technology changes how work is coordinated; it does not remove responsibility for evidence, people impact and authorized decisions.

## 1. What this two-hour sprint achieves

By the end you will:
1. Explain the general origins and purpose of this domain before naming a product.
2. Reconstruct its process evolution across **1975–2026**, identifying what improved and what remained unresolved.
3. Compare **2030, 2035, 2040 and 2050** scenarios, with a competing future and a validation question.
4. Map application responsibilities and source objects for the OmniVerse Energy case.
5. Produce a synthetic build prototype, run three tabletop tests and defend an evidence-based next step.

**The 20% to master:** journey versus screen; accessibility and frontline context; service ownership; authoritative communication; assisted success.

**Deliverable:** one Domain Evolution Build Card containing a timeline, persona needs, ownership map, state/control prototype, option decision and test evidence. Practical capability here means implementation preparation; it does not imply production configuration proficiency after two hours.

## 2. Origins and purpose — the general story

Employee service moved from personnel counters and notices toward self-service portals and digital journeys. Experience architecture asks whether people can complete the intended task in their real working context. A single screen can orchestrate services; it does not transfer all service ownership to the portal.

**Teach the decision first:** what decision is needed, whose outcome matters, what evidence supports it, which application is responsible, and who has authority to act?

### Fifty years of process evolution

Dates group broad historical patterns for learning. They do not claim invention dates, uniform adoption or a source-proven worldwide chronology. Paper, legacy, cloud and assisted processes can coexist today; the table is an educational synthesis.

| Era | Process pattern | What improved | Remaining gap | Energy-sector example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1975–1984 | Personnel counters, paper forms and noticeboards | Human assistance and local familiarity | Limited hours and unequal access | Shift workers miss daytime HR support |
| 1985–1994 | Telephone support and electronic office communication | Broader coordination | Desk-based channels exclude field populations | A rig worker needs a suitable service route |
| 1995–2004 | Intranets and employee self-service | Direct access to information and transactions | Multiple credentials and inaccessible forms | Finding a payslip requires the right system and permission |
| 2005–2014 | HR portals and case management | More traceable service requests | Case closure may not mean resolution | Track employee outcome, not just ticket status |
| 2015–2019 | Mobile experience and journey orchestration | Services closer to field work | Connectivity, accessibility and private-device assumptions | Provide usable low-connectivity and assisted options |
| 2020–2022 | Remote support and frontline inclusion | More focus on distributed work | Digital-only design still excludes users | Design for shift constraints and safe device use |
| 2023–2026 | Conversational and proactive assistance | Potentially less navigation and repetition | Wrong answers, notification overload and premature closure | Use authoritative policies and clear human handoff |

**Evolution activity:** cover the table. For each era, say “the process changed from ___ to ___ because ___; the unresolved problem was ___.” Use an energy example rather than a product slogan.

**Historical caution:** the 2020–2022 row describes possible process pressures/responses in distributed work, not proof that every energy enterprise changed in that way. Modern product documentation supports current capabilities, not the full historical chronology.

## 3. Future perspectives to 2050

**Scenario boundary:** all four horizons are possible design directions. None is an official SAP roadmap, forecast, legal prediction or achieved OmniVerse result.

| Horizon | Possible process direction | What to validate before scaling |
|---|---|---|
| 2030 | Accessible assistance across approved service channels | Test a bounded synthetic pilot; compare with a non-AI/manual option and preserve an accountable owner |
| 2035 | Personalized journeys with minimal data and worker choice | Validate evidence quality, worker access and role/country differences; do not infer authority from a recommendation |
| 2040 | Coordinated services across employment moments | Evaluate interoperability, practical operating value, worker impact and recovery under changing requirements |
| 2050 | A possible inclusive experience ecosystem preserving authoritative safety channels and human care | Retain human challenge, explainable responsibility, accessible alternatives and evidence history; revise when assumptions fail |

| Alternative 2050 world | What could happen | Design choice useful today |
|---|---|---|
| Trusted learning ecosystem | People, accountable owners and bounded assistance improve the capability together | Inspectable evidence, worker voice and measured experiments |
| Opaque metric/automation regime | Convenient scores or actions displace context and responsibility | Explicit prohibitions, review gates, correction and fallback |
| Fragmented local ecosystems | Different applications, definitions and institutions remain incompatible | Shared semantics and acceptance contracts with justified local variants |

**2050 experiment question:** which part of the future could you test with today's synthetic records? What observation would make you abandon the proposed design?

## 4. OmniVerse Energy — the decision and the people

**Fictional enterprise baseline:** OmniVerse Energy Ltd., 68,000 employees across 42 countries. “OmniEnergy” is an alias preserved in original research. Historical eras are not a corporate biography; OmniVerse was founded in 1978.

**Create one inclusive digital front door for the rig, refinery, power plant, wind farm, retail station, field, and office.**
OmniEnergy employees navigate multiple portals, credentials, help channels, policy repositories, and mobile tools. Critical communications reach desk workers more reliably than shift and field populations, while common leave, payslip, schedule, learning, and support journeys create avoidable effort.

**Scope note:** The portal is not an emergency-control system. Safety-critical messages retain approved operations channels and fallback; normal HR notifications must not compete with them without agreed priority.

## 6. Key concepts and application responsibility

| Concept | Meaning and boundary |
|---|---|
| Journey | End-to-end user purpose across services |
| Task success | Intended outcome, not page visit or chatbot answer |
| Accessibility | Usable interaction for differing needs; verify with people |
| Assisted success | Human-supported completion counted transparently |
| Notification priority | Governed urgency/channel and acknowledgement |
| Case resolution | Confirmed service outcome; not automated closure alone |

| Application / accountable area | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Experience front door | Navigation and orchestration |
| HR service owner | Case resolution and authoritative response |
| Source transaction application | Approved leave/pay/learning state |
| Communications | Targeting and campaign coordination |
| Safety/operations channels | Authoritative emergency instructions and escalation |

**Source information objects:** service case; journey; language; accessibility need; notification priority; feedback. Record attribute owner, authoritative source, purpose, access, retention, effective date and quality rule.

**Source value stream:** Need → discover service → authenticate → act → track → resolve → feedback. Define a stakeholder-valued exit criterion at each stage before detailed process modeling.

A capability is an ability the organization needs; the value stream describes progression toward stakeholder value. Neither is simply a list of applications.

**AA boundary:** this sprint assigns application responsibility and acceptance criteria. Reference data stewardship, security, integration, UX, infrastructure and AI concerns without assuming the application view alone implements those controls. Cross-domain consumers must not silently become owners of source facts.

## 8. Build experiment — a small, reviewable implementation design

Use **two synthetic records**, one routine and one variant drawn from the source challenges. Assign teaching identifiers **HR10-SYN-001/002**. Do not use real employee information, internal endpoints or live banking/access actions.

| Build Card field | What you must write |
|---|---|
| Purpose/population | Decision, affected person/role and intended outcome; identify missing facts |
| Two variants | Low connectivity / inaccessible task versus Conflicting alerts |
| Objects and time | Identifiers, source, relevant validity/as-of/processing dates; distinguish their meanings |
| Lifecycle/gates | Proposed from-state → permitted transition → prerequisite → accountable actor |
| Evidence | Source reference, validation status, version and correction owner |
| Application boundary | Experience front door responsibility versus Safety/operations channels |
| Global/local decision | Shared principle, justified role/country variant and evidence still needed |
| Human and automation boundary | One permitted administrative task; one protected consequential decision |
| Exception/recovery | Visible exception, owner, fallback, recovery and confirmation |
| Architecture decision | Compare more portals, one monolithic replacement and a governed front door that orchestrates accountable services. |
| Measure | Definition, denominator, exclusions, as-of date, owner and interpretation limits |
| Test evidence | Input → expected → actual paper result → correction → evidence note → pass/revise |

**Conceptual workflow:** establish approved source evidence → validate → apply agreed rule → authorized decision/action → confirm downstream business outcome → review exceptions. These are teaching stages, not prescribed SAP object/status names. Choose actual module configuration only after validating the contract and product version.

### Three tabletop tests

| Test | Challenge | Expected result in this proposed design |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Low connectivity / inaccessible task | Show status, preserve safe progress and offer accessible assisted path; do not falsely confirm completion |
| T2 | Conflicting alerts | Apply agreed priority and authoritative channel; critical safety communication cannot rely solely on generic HR portal reach |
| T3 | Sensitive case automation | Escalate to authorized human with minimal relevant context; employee can see next steps |

**How to run:** represent each record/state on paper or in a simple table. Execute the steps yourself; record whether the initial design produced the expected outcome. If it did not, revise and rerun. Designed expectations are not actual SAP test results.

**Acceptance:** explain the timeline, defend one 2050 hypothesis against an alternative, distinguish the concepts, assign owners and satisfy all three tabletop expectations after documented revision. An unsupported safety/pay/access/employee-rights outcome requires redesign. List any control that remains untested.

**Source measure:** Task success = completed intended journeys / attempted journeys; segment frontline and office cohorts and record assisted success. Owner: domain lead unless specified; cadence: proposed pilot review and monthly follow-up. Establish denominator, exclusions, as-of date and baseline before setting a numeric target. Original source “Victory Measures” are targets, not achievements.

Do not fabricate a baseline, achieved benefit or target. A successful prototype proves only the limited behaviour you actually exercised.
