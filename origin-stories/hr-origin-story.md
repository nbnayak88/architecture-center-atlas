# The Origin Story of Human Resources
## From managing records to enabling human potential
SuccessLabs Academy | Origin Story Sprint | HR-OS-001

## One clear promise
By the end of this sprint, explain how HR enables people and enterprise outcomes, and redesign one employee experience with clear ownership and evidence.

## The opening scene
It is Asha's first day. Her manager expects her at a customer meeting. Her laptop is not ready. Payroll has a different joining date. Asha has completed three forms asking for the same information.

Mira, the HR partner, sees an onboarding task. Dev, the manager, sees lost time. Asha wonders whether she belongs.

One employee's first morning reveals an enterprise: decisions, data, technology, trust and care. HR starts with a person. Its impact extends to families, teams, customers and communities.

Asha, Mira and Dev are fictional teaching personas. This is not a reported client incident.

## The path up the mountain
These stages are a teaching lens, not a universal chronology. Organisations may operate across several stages at once.

### 1. The foothills: keeping accurate records
Employment records, attendance and pay establish basic reliability. A wrong joining date can affect multiple downstream decisions.
- Mindset shift: an employee record represents a person's livelihood.
- Curiosity question: what happens when the same fact has three different values?
- Experiment: trace Asha's joining date through HR, payroll and access provisioning.

### 2. The first climb: making work dependable and fair
Policies, responsibilities and consistent decisions help people understand what to expect. A policy becomes useful when people can apply it to a real situation.
- Mindset shift: design clarity into everyday decisions.
- Curiosity question: where does consistency help, and where is judgment required?
- Experiment: define the owner, escalation and completion evidence for a delayed first-day setup.

### 3. The connected trail: linking the employee journey
Recruiting, onboarding, employee data, time, payroll and benefits depend on shared information and coordinated action.
- Mindset shift: own the handoff as well as the task.
- Curiosity question: can a successful HR transaction still produce a poor employee experience?
- Experiment: map the path from accepted offer to first correct pay; identify repeated entry and broken handoffs.

### 4. The wider view: growing capability
Learning, feedback, career opportunities and rewards shape what people can contribute. Completing a course alone does not demonstrate that someone can perform a task.
- Mindset shift: develop capability and create opportunities to apply it.
- Curiosity question: what can Asha do after learning that she could not do before?
- Experiment: define one role capability, one practice task and one observable performance criterion.

### 5. The exposed ridge: building trust and adaptability
Change, workforce disruption and uneven access to opportunity reveal whether the organisation's stated values work in practice.
- Mindset shift: assess who benefits, who struggles and who is missing.
- Curiosity question: does the redesigned experience work for a remote employee, a shift worker and someone needing accessibility support?
- Experiment: test the same onboarding journey with these three situations; propose one improvement for each.

### 6. The horizon: technology that supports accountable human judgment
In a speculative scenario through 2050, assistants could coordinate routine tasks, explain options and identify missing information. Decisions affecting employment, pay and opportunity still require clear accountability, appropriate review and ways to challenge errors.
- Mindset shift: automate with a defined purpose and limits.
- Curiosity question: what should an assistant recommend, execute or escalate?
- Experiment: design an onboarding assistant. Specify permitted data, allowed actions, escalation triggers, audit evidence and a stop mechanism. Do not delegate hiring rejection, pay changes or employment termination in this exercise.

## Understand the whole journey
1. Plan the work and capabilities needed.
2. Attract and select people using clear criteria.
3. Prepare onboarding, access and role expectations.
4. Maintain reliable employment information.
5. Coordinate time, pay and benefits.
6. Support feedback, performance and development.
7. Enable growth, mobility and succession.
8. Manage changes and exits with clear responsibilities.
9. Learn from employee feedback and operational evidence.

This journey connects employee experience with business capability. It is broader than a software module.

## A 120-minute Origin Story Sprint
| Time | Learner action | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 minutes | Experience Asha's first morning; predict the consequences | Three pain points |
| 10–25 minutes | Explore the six stages and challenge assumptions | One mindset shift |
| 25–45 minutes | Map accepted offer to first correct pay | Employee journey map |
| 45–65 minutes | Find one root cause and redesign the handoff | Before/after process |
| 65–85 minutes | Test remote, shift and accessibility situations | Exception and inclusion notes |
| 85–105 minutes | Define an assistant's actions and limits | Delegation boundary sheet |
| 105–120 minutes | Demonstrate the redesign and defend a trade-off | Five-minute explanation |

## Measure the transformation
Submit one employee journey map, one improved handoff, one exception route and one five-minute explanation.

A peer should be able to identify:
- The employee need and intended outcome.
- An accountable owner for every step.
- Required information and its authoritative source.
- Evidence that the step is complete.
- An escalation route when the normal path fails.

Count repeated data entries, unclear owners and clarification rounds in the first simulation. Repeat after redesign and report the observed difference. These are learning results, not claimed production savings.

## Keep the thread traceable
| ID | Origin-story need | Design response | Test evidence | Readiness check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HR-OS-N01 | Asha needs access before starting work | Named access owner and readiness checklist | Simulate a joiner with missing approval | Owner confirms access and exception route |
| HR-OS-N02 | Asha needs one reliable joining date | Approved source and controlled propagation | Check an authorised date change across dependent records | Reconcile sample records |
| HR-OS-N03 | Asha needs confidence about her first pay | Payroll handoff with completeness checks | Simulate missing required information | Payroll owner confirms exception handling |

The origin story explains why a requirement exists. Later, requirement gathering, architecture, testing and go-live evidence can refer to the same need ID. This table introduces traceability; it is not a complete compliance or payroll control design.

## Twelve architecture lenses
| Lens | A question to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| Enterprise Architect | How does the workforce enable enterprise priorities? |
| Business Architect | Which capabilities, roles and decisions support the journey? |
| Integration Architect | Which events connect HR, payroll, identity and other systems? |
| Domain Architect | What do employee, worker, job and position mean here? |
| Cloud & Infrastructure Architect | What availability and recovery does the journey need? |
| Application & Process Architect | Which application owns each process responsibility? |
| AI Architect | How will assistance be evaluated, limited and reviewed? |
| Security Architect | Who may access or change employee information? |
| Industry Architect | How do sector work patterns change the design? |
| Data Architect | Which source owns each fact, and how is quality checked? |
| UI/UX Architect | Can people understand and complete the experience accessibly? |
| Technology Architect | What technical choices support interoperability and maintainability? |

## Closing invitation
Asha did not arrive asking for an HR system. She arrived ready to contribute.

Choose one moment where a person cannot do their best work. Discover what blocks them, connect the decisions behind it and run a small experiment that improves the experience.

That is the starting point for architecting experiences for a better world.
